02SketchMind

Supporting algorithmic reasoning through executable sketching

// ~40 s. Four beats. Tell it as stages: build, get support, get feedback, refine. SketchMind supports each stage of this process. First, she builds the algorithm by sketching. With executable tracing, every pen stroke runs an operation and becomes a step. [1] When steps repeat, she merges them into her own reusable pen. [2] Multi-level trace views show the same steps as values, as positions, and as a general pattern. [3] When she thinks she is done, SketchMind gives feedback: a counterexample, an input where her plan fails. [4] And she refines her plan, turning her steps into a loop. Let's see this in the system.

// ~50 s. Twenty-one quick beats; keep moving, one short phrase per click. For an audience new to SMT. How does SketchMind find it? We use an SMT solver, which finds numbers that satisfy a list of conditions. The unknowns are a0 to a3. [1] Next to her plan, we run the standard Bubble Sort. She is missing passes 2 and 3. [2] Each step gives a condition: a0 > a1… [3] …a0 ≤ a2… [4] …a2 > a3. [5] Pass 2… [6] … [7] …and pass 3. [8] Her pass 1 stays exactly as it is. [14] Only a0 > a3 works: pass 2 must swap, and pass 2 is what she is missing. 03Implementation

Three layers, one loop.

// Slide 14. Three beats. Briefly, the implementation: three layers in one loop. The sketch layer turns handwriting into an array and strokes into operations. Gemini recognizes the digits; for a stroke, what counts is which elements it crosses, not its shape. [1] The execution engine keeps a versioned sequence of steps and replays them, with her own ink. [2] The symbolic engine compiles her trace to SMT-LIB and checks it with Z3. [3] If her plan is correct, she's done. If not, the counterexample comes back as a new instance, and she replays her sketch on it.

// ~55 s. Three beats. Card 1 is SketchMind's solver; cards 2 and 3 are alternatives, not implemented. All checked by enumeration. I'd like to end with three open questions. First: is the nearest counterexample the clearest one? Her one pass sorts 3, 1, 7, 4. Every input here keeps her compares the same and breaks her plan. SketchMind changes as few numbers as possible, then by as little as possible: the last number, 4 becomes 2. [1] Another option keeps her numbers and only changes their order: swap 3 and 4. [2] Or we keep all her numbers and add a new one at the end, for example a 2. [3] Our choice is a heuristic, not a definition of a good counterexample. Which one makes her missing step easiest to see? We don't know yet, and we think it is worth studying.
